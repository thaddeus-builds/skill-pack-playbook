# Remaking one reference video: what says a layer carries the value, and what an AI person may do in it

Researched 2026-11-04. Asked by the owner on issue #527 (the single-reference remake as user insight's second consumer): is anything worth adding to the method the issue proposes? Kept research was read first (`tools/research.py` over adcut, video-recipes, product-image-to-video, ad-copy, koc-script, video-ads, meta-ads and five more); video-recipes' scan of 2026-11-02 already covers copying, AI labels and the teardown tools, so it is cited, not repeated. Then four searches, one per lens, and three pages of US federal rules read through a mirror.

## Verdict

1. **"An AI person never speaks a review" is too narrow.** The US rule's consumer testimonial covers "depictions of the name, signature, likeness" of a person that viewers are likely to believe reflects the experience of someone who bought or used the product (16 CFR 465.1(f)). A business may not create one that misrepresents that the person exists or used the product (465.2(a)). So an AI person filmed as a customer (first person, handheld, "my bike", unboxing, before and after) is a testimonial even if it says nothing about the product. The endorsement guides allow actors in the role of consumers only with a clear and conspicuous disclosure that they are not actual consumers (16 CFR 255.2(c)). The owner's rule of 2026-11-04 (the proof half is a real customer with a release) is stricter than that and stays; the method should add that the *inspiration* half may show an AI person only as a character in a story, never framed as a customer. **[documented]** for the rule text, **[inference]** for the framing test.
2. **The rule is being enforced.** The 2024 order against an AI review-writing tool was set aside in December 2025, but the same month FTC staff sent warning letters to ten companies under the reviews rule, and law firms describe 2026 as its enforcement period, with AI-made reviews and scripted endorsements named as areas of interest. A change in how the agency treats AI tools did not change the rule. **[documented]** (the set-aside) and **[vendor]** (law-firm reports of the letters)
3. **For a paid reference, run length and variants are better evidence than engagement.** Creative strategists' guides agree that the strongest public signal of an ad that works is how long it has run and how many variants its advertiser made of it; they also say it shows continued spend, not return. A platform's per-second curve on a top ad, where one is shown, is the only public view of *which second* holds people (video-recipes, lens 1, second-hand). Engagement against the account's median stays the weakest signal for an organic post. **[vendor]**
4. **A near 1:1 copy of a competitor's video can advertise the competitor.** Trade research on brand linkage says an ad whose audience cannot link it to its brand is read as a category ad or as the leader's, and that carrying a competitor's own cues is worse than carrying generic ones. No primary study was read (below), so this is a direction, not a number. **[vendor]**
5. **Value needs a test to become more than a hypothesis.** The issue already limits the options to a replica and one variant that changes a single layer. That pair is an A/B test of that layer; its result is the only evidence that turns a layer's `carries_value` from a person's judgement into a measurement. **[inference]**

## What follows for the method page and `remake.brief/1`

All **[inference]**, each naming what it changes:

- **The layer list** gains the layers adcut already codes and the issue's list lacks: the promise (`value_prop`), the proof (a demonstration, a number, a testimonial), the offer and call to act, and the brand (when and how the brand and the product appear). The issue's nine layers are mostly form; these four are where a viewer's reason to buy sits.
- **The evidence kinds** for `carries_value` are a closed list in the contract: `run_days` and `variants` (a paid ad, from an ad library), `curve` (a per-second curve the platform showed), `engagement_vs_median`, `comments`, `client_words`, and later `test` (our own A/B result). Frequency across videos is not on the list, so the contract cannot carry it as evidence.
- **The proof layer** records its framing: `story` (a character; AI allowed under the owner's rules and each market's AI label) or `customer` (first-person or review-style; a real person with a release, or a placeholder that blocks delivery). The delivery gate reads the framing, not only the words.
- **"Never copy" per reference:** the brief lists the reference's distinctive cues (a colour, a mascot, a sound logo, a catchphrase, a signature shot) and its lines, music, faces and voices as things the remake does not take, and names where the client's brand and product appear in the first seconds. This follows video-recipes' rule "structure only" and differs from adcut's R58, whose replica card is near 1:1 in wording; the owner settles which applies to a competitor's video.
- **The test result goes back:** the variant's result is written on the layer as evidence `test`, so the next brief for the same product and segment starts from it.

## What each source says, and what does not transfer

- **US 16 CFR Part 465 (consumer reviews and testimonials), sections 465.1 and 465.2.** Defines a consumer testimonial to include depictions of a likeness; forbids creating a review or testimonial that misrepresents that the person exists, used the product, or what their experience was. *Transfers:* verdict 1, the `customer` framing and the release. *Does not transfer:* it is US law; other markets need their own row (China's AI labels and the EU's Art. 50 are in video-recipes' lens 3).
- **US 16 CFR 255.2(c) and example 6 (endorsement guides).** People represented as actual consumers should be actual consumers, or the ad clearly discloses that they are not; employed actors are disclosed. *Transfers:* the legal floor under the owner's stricter rule. *Does not transfer:* the guides are guidance, not a rule with penalties of their own.
- **FTC, the AI review-tool case (complaint 2024, order set aside 2025-12) and the warning letters (2025-12).** *Transfers:* verdict 2. *Does not transfer:* the case was about selling a tool, not about an advertiser's own film.
- **Creative strategists' competitor-ad guides (several vendors, 2025 and 2026).** Run length (often "30 days or more") and the number of variants as public proxies; benchmarks of an ad's life disagree by platform and source. *Transfers:* the evidence kinds `run_days` and `variants`. *Does not transfer:* the vendors' thresholds and phase labels, which are not published methods; an organic post has neither signal.
- **Trade press on brand linkage and misattribution (marketing columns citing Ehrenberg-Bass and System1 work).** *Transfers:* the "never copy" list and the brand in the first seconds. *Does not transfer:* the percentages quoted second-hand.
